How a derivation works

  1. Read one idea at a time. Each step is a single screen and a single new key term. Scroll to take the next one.
  2. Never more than four at once. Working memory holds about four chunks, so after four the feed stops at a milestone and you rebuild them before anything new arrives.
  3. The next step is the question. There is no separate practice question. You are asked what follows, and answering it teaches the next term.
  4. Chains branch. A concept can rest on several chains. At a milestone you drag terms into each chain, and the chains meet at the concept they lead to.
  5. Prerequisites first, any order. Chains can be scheduled in any order, but every direct prerequisite has to be met before the concept it leads to.
  6. The whole derivation is the final test. At the concept you fill every term of the whole graph from memory, and the finished graph becomes a new, growing branch of the knowledge map.
